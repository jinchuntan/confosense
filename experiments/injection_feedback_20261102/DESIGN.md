# Where a benchmark inserts sensor faults

Frozen before inspecting the paired fixed-forecast outcomes or the delayed-input outcomes. The histories, original rolling scores and earlier recovery experiment were already known. This is a retrospective comparison on two BDG2 cohorts.

1. Give a detector exactly the same sequence of disturbed test targets under two evaluation protocols. In the first protocol, compute forecasts from original meter histories once, then score the disturbed readings. In the second, let those readings enter each subsequent forecast just as they would online. Keep thresholds, model, buildings, matching window, and every event placement identical. The original stream and its alarm workload are identical by construction.
2. Primary comparison: separate-tail CQR, numerical flags, validation budget 0.5 background episodes per meter-day, both original cohorts, fitting seed 42, all periods and catalogues. Report event-level switches in both directions, their sum over effective injections, net difference in credited recall and first onsets during the disturbance. Report combined flags, the other three policies, all budgets, site variation and disturbance families. Show the full transition between no first alarm, first alarm before change, during the change span, and after the final changed reading. Distinguish effects of the entire disturbed history from the prior single-event intervention.
3. Practical test: fixed four-hour delay on the most recent meter inputs (latest input at t-5 rather than t-1) with identical calendar, day/week seasonal lags, and last available weather. Refit and recalibrate the same quantile forecaster on the same training support. Compare numerical separate-tail CQR at the 0.5 selection budget with the original live model and the scaled-quantile baseline. No timing or fault labels go to the delayed model. Test periods may achieve different background workloads. Report all families and both cohorts. Seek a higher share of first alarms during the disturbance at comparable achieved workload (within 0.05 episode/meter-day) in both cohorts. A failure rules out recommending this simple configuration. The chosen delay is informed by this benchmark's four-reading slot and needs testing on other durations.
4. Withhold universal priority, real-equipment, or deployment-benefit claims. All methods share one BDG2 source, short synthetic events, and history-based forecast family. Event- and site-weighted quantities are descriptive. A future cross-dataset test must vary disturbance duration and include an appropriately labeled or controlled external building stream.
